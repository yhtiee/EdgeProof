An uptrend keeps making higher lows. When price falls below the most recent swing low, that pattern is broken. Traders call this a break of structure.

It doesn't mean the trend has reversed. It means the simple description "higher highs and higher lows" is no longer true for now.

---

A break can lead to three things:

- A **reversal**: the market starts making lower highs and lower lows.
- A **range**: price moves sideways for a while.
- A **false break**: price dips below the low, then quickly recovers and the trend carries on.

From the chart alone, you can't tell in advance which one you're seeing.

---

This is where vague words cause trouble. "Price broke the low" can mean a wick poked below it, or a candle closed below it. Those are different events, with different results.

A testable rule says exactly which: for example, "a 4-hour candle closes below the most recent swing low".

---

- [ ] Yes, because price went below the low
- [x] No, because the candle closed above the low

---

::takeaway
A break of structure ends the current pattern, but doesn't tell you what comes next. Define a break precisely, by close or by wick, so your rule can be tested.
::
