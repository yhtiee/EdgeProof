The [[timeframe]] you trade decides how often you need to look at charts, how long trades last, and how big your stops are.

- **Lower timeframes** (1 to 15 minutes): many trades, short holds, small stops, lots of screen time.
- **Middle timeframes** (1 to 4 hours): trades lasting hours to days.
- **Higher timeframes** (daily and weekly): fewer trades, longer holds, bigger stops, little screen time.

---

Your onboarding plan suggested a style based on your free hours and the [[session|sessions]] you can watch. That's the first filter: a strategy you can't follow at the times you're free won't be followed.

---

Lower timeframes have two extra costs. First, costs: a spread is a far bigger share of a 10-pip stop than a 100-pip one. Second, noise: small charts move randomly a lot, and it's harder to tell a real move from a wiggle.

Try the cost calculator with a 10-pip stop and then a 60-pip stop.

::takeaway
Pick a timeframe that fits the time you actually have. Lower timeframes demand more screen time and pay more in costs and noise; higher ones need patience and bigger stops.
::
