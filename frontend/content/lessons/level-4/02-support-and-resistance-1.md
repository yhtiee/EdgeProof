[[support|Support]] is a price area where falling prices have stopped before, because buyers stepped in. [[resistance|Resistance]] is an area where rising prices have stopped, because sellers stepped in.

These ideas go back to the earliest books on chart analysis [@edwards-magee-stock-trends].

---

Why might prices react at the same areas again? Partly memory: traders remember where price turned, and place orders there. Partly round numbers like 1.1000, where many orders gather.

Neither is a law. Sometimes price stops at a level; often it goes straight through.

---

Think of support and resistance as **zones**, not exact lines. Price rarely turns at precisely the same pip twice. A zone 10 to 20 pips wide on a daily chart is more realistic than a single line.

---

::question{type="mc"}
Price has turned up from around 1.0700 three times. What's the most realistic way to describe that?
- [ ] 1.0700 is a level price can't go below
- [x] There's a support zone around 1.0700 that buyers have defended before
- [ ] Price will turn up from 1.0700 next time
? Past reactions describe a zone where buyers have been active. They don't guarantee the next reaction.
::

---

::takeaway
Support and resistance are zones where price has turned before. They're useful for planning entries and stops, but they're tendencies, not walls, and need testing like any other idea.
::
