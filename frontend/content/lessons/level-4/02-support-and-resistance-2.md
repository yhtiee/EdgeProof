When price breaks through a resistance zone, that old ceiling often becomes a floor: later dips stop near it. The same happens the other way, with broken support becoming resistance. This is called role reversal [@murphy-technical-analysis].

---

Not every break holds. Price often pokes through a zone, triggers the orders gathered there, and then snaps back. That's a false break, sometimes called a fakeout.

Traders who buy every break get caught by false ones. Traders who always bet against breaks get caught by real ones.

---

So how do you tell them apart? In the moment, you can't with certainty. You can only define your rule clearly and test how often it works:

- What counts as a break? A close beyond the zone? By how many pips?
- Do you enter on the break, or wait for a retest of the zone?
- Where does your [[stop-loss]] go if it's false?

---

::question{type="mc"}
A trader wants to buy breaks above resistance. Which version of the rule can be tested?
- [ ] "Buy when the break looks strong"
- [x] "Buy when a 1-hour candle closes at least 5 pips above the zone, with a stop below the zone"
- [ ] "Buy breaks unless it feels like a fakeout"
? Only the second gives the same answer every time. "Looks strong" and "feels like" can't be tested, because they change with your mood.
::

---

::takeaway
Broken resistance often turns into support and the other way round, but false breaks are common. Write exact break and stop rules, then let testing show how often they work.
::
