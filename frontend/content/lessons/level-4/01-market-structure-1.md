A price chart is a picture of a price over time. Time runs from left to right along the bottom. Price runs from low to high up the side. The newest price is always on the right.

---

The simplest chart is a line joining the closing prices. Most traders use [[candlestick|candlesticks]] instead, because each candle shows four prices for its period: where it opened, its highest point, its lowest point, and where it closed.

You'll learn to read candles in detail later in this level.

---

Every chart has a [[timeframe]]: how much time each candle covers. On a 1-hour chart, each candle is one hour. On a daily chart, each candle is one day.

The same price history looks very different on different timeframes. A sharp drop on a 5-minute chart can be a tiny dip on a daily one.

---

::question{type="mc"}
On a 4-hour chart, what does each candle show?
- [ ] The price at exactly 4 o'clock
- [x] The open, high, low and close for one 4-hour period
- [ ] The average price over 4 days
? Each candle covers one period of the chart's timeframe. On a 4-hour chart, that's four hours of trading.
::

---

::takeaway
Time runs left to right, price bottom to top, newest on the right. Each candle covers one period of the chart's timeframe and shows its open, high, low and close.
::
