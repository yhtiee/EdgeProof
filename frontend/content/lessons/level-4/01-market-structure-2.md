Prices rarely move in straight lines. They zig-zag: up, pull back, up again. Each turning point leaves a mark.

- A **swing high** is a peak: a high with lower highs on both sides.
- A **swing low** is a valley: a low with higher lows on both sides.

---

Joining the swing points shows the market's structure:

- **Higher highs and higher lows:** an uptrend. Each push up goes further, and each pullback stops higher.
- **Lower highs and lower lows:** a downtrend.
- **Highs and lows at similar levels:** a range. Price moves sideways between two areas.

This is the classic way of describing a [[trend]], found in standard textbooks on chart analysis [@murphy-technical-analysis].

---

Here's the honest part. Which swing points "count" is a judgement call. Is a 3-pip dip a swing low, or just noise? Different traders will draw different structures on the same chart.

That's fine for learning to see. For a strategy you can test, you'll later turn it into a rule, such as "a swing low has at least 3 higher lows on each side".

---

::question{type="mc"}
A chart shows swing highs at 1.0820, 1.0860 and 1.0905, and swing lows at 1.0790, 1.0830 and 1.0870. What's the structure?
- [x] An uptrend: higher highs and higher lows
- [ ] A downtrend: lower highs and lower lows
- [ ] A range: highs and lows at the same levels
? Each high is above the last, and each low is above the last. That's higher highs and higher lows.
::

---

::takeaway
Swing highs and lows are the market's turning points. Higher highs and lows describe an uptrend, lower ones a downtrend, and level ones a range. Turn "what counts" into a rule before testing.
::
