"Trade with the trend" is the most repeated advice in trading. It's only useful once you can say exactly what the trend is.

There are three common ways to define one.

---

**1. By structure:** higher highs and higher lows for an uptrend, as you learned earlier in this level.

**2. By a [[moving average]]:** for example, an uptrend while price closes above its 50-day average, or while a faster average is above a slower one.

**3. By trendline:** a line joining two or more swing lows (uptrend) or highs (downtrend). Trendlines are popular but subjective: two people rarely draw the same line [@murphy-technical-analysis].

---

Each definition gives slightly different answers on the same chart, especially at turning points. None is "correct". What matters is picking one, writing it down precisely, and using it the same way every time.

---

::question{type="mc"}
Which trend definition can be tested without any judgement?
- [ ] "The trend is up when the chart looks bullish"
- [x] "The trend is up when the daily close is above the 50-day simple moving average"
- [ ] "The trend is up when my trendline points up"
? The moving-average rule gives one clear answer for every day. The other two depend on what you see or where you draw.
::

---

::takeaway
A trend can be defined by structure, moving averages or trendlines. Choose one definition, write it precisely, and apply it the same way every time.
::
