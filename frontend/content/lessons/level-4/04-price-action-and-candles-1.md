Candlestick charts come from Japan, where rice traders used them centuries ago. Steve Nison's book introduced them to many Western traders [@nison-candlesticks].

Each [[candlestick]] packs four prices into one shape.

---

- The **body** is the thick part, between the open and the close.
- The **wicks** (or shadows) are the thin lines above and below. They reach the period's highest and lowest prices.
- If the close is above the open, it's a **rising** candle. If below, a **falling** candle. Charts show them in different colours.

---

Build your own candle. Move the open, close, high and low and watch the shape change.

::simulator{kind="candlestick"}

---

A candle tells a short story. A long lower wick means price fell during the period, then buyers pushed it back up. A small body with long wicks on both sides means a tug-of-war with no winner.

---

::question{type="mc"}
A candle opens at 1.0850, falls to 1.0820, rises to 1.0870, and closes at 1.0865. What kind of candle is it?
- [x] A rising candle with a body of 15 pips
- [ ] A falling candle with a body of 30 pips
- [ ] A rising candle with a body of 50 pips
? The close (1.0865) is above the open (1.0850): a rising candle. The body runs from 1.0850 to 1.0865, which is 15 pips.
::

---

::takeaway
Each candle shows open, high, low and close. The body spans open to close; the wicks reach the high and low. Its shape summarises who was in control during that period.
::
