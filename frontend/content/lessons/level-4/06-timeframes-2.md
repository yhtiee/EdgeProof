Zoom into any chart far enough and it looks like a random scribble. That randomness is called noise. The lower the timeframe, the bigger the noise compared with the real moves you're trying to trade.

---

A common beginner pattern: on a 5-minute chart, everything looks like a breakout or a reversal. Each one feels urgent. Most are noise, and acting on them all means many trades, many costs, and many decisions made in a hurry.

---

A simple habit helps: before looking at your trading timeframe, look one or two timeframes higher. A "breakdown" on a 15-minute chart might be a small pullback inside a clear uptrend on the 4-hour chart.

You'll learn a structured way to combine timeframes in Level 7.

---

::question{type="mc"}
On a 5-minute chart, EUR/USD seems to be collapsing. On the daily chart, it's in the middle of a steady uptrend. What's the most balanced reading?
- [ ] The uptrend is over
- [x] It may be a short-term move inside a bigger uptrend; the timeframes describe different things
- [ ] The daily chart is out of date
? Both charts are true at once: they describe different time spans. Higher timeframes give context to moves on lower ones.
::

---

::takeaway
Lower timeframes carry more noise. Checking a higher timeframe first gives context, so a small move isn't mistaken for a big change.
::
