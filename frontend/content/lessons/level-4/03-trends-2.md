Markets don't trend all the time. They switch between trending periods and ranges, where price moves sideways between support and resistance.

---

Strategies usually suit one condition:

- **Trend-following** strategies try to catch long moves. They tend to lose small amounts in ranges, while waiting for a trend.
- **Range** strategies buy near support and sell near resistance. They tend to get hurt when a range breaks into a trend.

Neither is better. They make money at different times.

---

The mistake is switching styles every week based on the last few days. Because you only know a trend or range in hindsight, you end up always one step behind.

A tested strategy has a defined condition for when it trades, and stays out the rest of the time.

::takeaway
Markets alternate between trends and ranges, and most strategies suit one of them. Define when your strategy trades, and judge it over many trades, not a single phase.
::
