Every country, or group of countries, uses its own money. That money is called a currency. Nigeria uses the naira, the United States uses the dollar, and most of Europe uses the euro.

A shop in Lagos wants naira. A shop in New York wants dollars. So when people, companies or governments buy something from another country, they first swap one currency for another.

---

The price of one currency in another is called an **exchange rate**.

Imagine the rate is 1 US dollar = 1,500 naira. That means you'd hand over 1,500 naira to get 1 dollar. It also means 1 naira is worth a small part of a dollar.

Exchange rates change all the time, because the number of people who want each currency keeps changing.

---

::question{type="numeric" answer="30000" tolerance="0" unit="naira"}
Imagine 1 US dollar costs 1,500 naira. How many naira do you need for 20 dollars?
? 20 dollars × 1,500 naira per dollar = 30,000 naira.
::

---

If the rate moves from 1,500 to 1,600 naira per dollar, each dollar now costs more naira. The dollar has become stronger against the naira, and the naira weaker against the dollar.

That's the whole idea behind the foreign exchange market, often shortened to **forex** or **FX**: people swapping currencies at rates that keep moving.

---

::question{type="mc"}
The rate moves from 1,500 to 1,600 naira per dollar. What happened?
- [ ] The naira got stronger against the dollar
- [x] The dollar got stronger against the naira
- [ ] Both currencies kept the same value
? Each dollar now costs 100 more naira, so the dollar buys more naira than before: it got stronger, and the naira got weaker.
::

---

::takeaway
An exchange rate is the price of one currency in another. When it moves, one currency has become stronger and the other weaker.
::
