Stock markets often rely on a single physical building or central exchange to process every buy and sell order. The foreign exchange market operates differently: it has no single central location, functioning instead as a decentralised network [@babypips-pipsology].

---

This structure is known as an over-the-counter market. Rather than routing trades through one central clearing house, participants transact directly with one another through an electronic network of banks, financial institutions, and retail [[broker|brokers]].

---

Because trading takes place across participants worldwide, the market is very large. Global FX trading averaged $9.6 trillion per day in April 2025 [@bis-triennial-2025].

---

Trading flows continuously across international financial centres during the business week [@babypips-pipsology]. As major centres in Asia close, European markets open, followed by North America, keeping activity moving 24 hours a day from Monday through Friday.

---

Because no central exchange sets a single official price, individual banks and liquidity providers broadcast their own quotes. This decentralised structure explains why the exact price and the [[spread]] offered by one [[broker]] can differ slightly from another.

::takeaway
Forex has no single central exchange. Trading passes between financial centres around the clock on weekdays, and prices can differ slightly from one broker to another.
