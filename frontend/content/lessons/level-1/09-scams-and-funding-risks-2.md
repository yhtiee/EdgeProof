A [[broker]] holds your money. Before you send them anything, check who regulates them: the government body that licenses them and sets their rules.

A good website or a famous sponsor isn't evidence. A regulator's public register is.

---

In Nigeria, the rules are clear. In April 2025 Nigeria's SEC said that operating an online forex trading platform without SEC registration is illegal under the Investments and Securities Act 2025 [@sec-nigeria-isa-2025-forex].

Other regulators run public registers and warning lists too, such as the UK's Financial Conduct Authority [@fca-scams].

---

**How to check, step by step:**

- Find the company's full legal name and licence number on its website.
- Look it up on the regulator's own website, not through a link the company sends you.
- Check the name, website address and licence number all match.
- Search the regulator's warning list for the company's name.

---

Regulation also decides your protections. EU and UK rules, for example, limit retail leverage and require [[negative balance protection]] [@fca-ps19-18]. A broker licensed only in a country with weak rules may offer neither.

::takeaway
Before depositing, find the broker on its regulator's public register, check the details match, and check the warning list. Where a broker is regulated decides which protections you get.
