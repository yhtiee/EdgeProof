Prices move in [[pip|pips]]. For most pairs a pip is the fourth decimal place: EUR/USD moving from 1.0850 to 1.0851 is one pip.

For pairs with the Japanese yen, like USD/JPY, a pip is the second decimal place: 151.20 to 151.21.

---

Trade size is measured in [[lot|lots]].

- A standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency.
- A mini lot is 10,000 units.
- A micro lot is 1,000 units.

**On EUR/USD, one pip on one standard lot is worth $10.** On a mini lot it's $1, and on a micro lot $0.10.

---

::question{type="numeric" answer="100" tolerance="0" unit="USD"}
You hold 0.5 lots of EUR/USD. The price moves 20 pips in your favour. How many dollars is that?
? 0.5 lots is worth $5 a pip. 20 pips × $5 = $100.
::

---

::question{type="numeric" answer="175" tolerance="0" unit="USD"}
Same trade, but the price moves 35 pips against you instead. How many dollars is that loss? Enter it as a positive number.
? 35 pips × $5 a pip = $175. Size works the same way in both directions.
::

---

::takeaway
Pip value grows with size. Double the lots and every pip is worth twice as much, against you as well as for you.
::
