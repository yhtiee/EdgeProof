Even a real, licensed company can get into trouble. When it does, the money it holds for you can be frozen, delayed or lost. This is called counterparty risk: the risk that the other side of your deal can't pay.

---

The Swiss franc jump in 2015 showed this. After that move, FXCM's clients owed it more than $225 million in negative balances [@cnbc-fxcm-2015], and the firm needed emergency funding to survive.

When a broker is hit that hard, clients can be affected even if their own trades were fine.

---

Prop firms carry this risk too. These are companies that sell trading challenges and promise to share profits if you pass.

In September 2023 the CFTC charged the prop firm My Forex Funds with fraudulently taking over $300 million from customers, and a court froze its assets [@cftc-my-forex-funds-2023]. Traders' accounts and pending payouts were frozen overnight.

---

That case later ended in an unusual way. In May 2025 a US federal judge dismissed the CFTC's case against My Forex Funds' parent with prejudice and sanctioned the CFTC after finding it had made false representations to the court [@willkie-my-forex-funds-2025].

The lesson stands either way: from the freeze in 2023 until the case ended in 2025, customers' money was out of reach.

---

::question{type="mc"}
What's counterparty risk?
- [ ] The risk that your trade idea is wrong
- [x] The risk that the firm holding your money can't or won't pay it
- [ ] The risk that a pair's spread widens at night
? Counterparty risk is about the firm, not the market: your money can be stuck if the broker or prop firm fails, freezes, or refuses to pay.
::

---

::takeaway
Your money is only as safe as the firm holding it. Use regulated firms, keep only what you need with any one of them, and treat prop firm fees as money you might not see again.
::
