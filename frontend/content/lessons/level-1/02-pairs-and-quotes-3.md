An exotic [[currency pair]] combines one major currency with the currency of a smaller or developing economy [@babypips-pipsology]. In these pairs, the major currency can serve as either the [[base currency]] or the [[quote currency]].

Developing or smaller economies often have financial markets that are less integrated into global commerce compared to major economies.

---

Common examples of exotic pairs include USD/TRY (US dollar and Turkish lira) or EUR/PLN (euro and Polish zloty).

Because there is no single official rule defining exotic pairs, a [[broker]] may classify them differently. A pair listed under exotics on one platform might appear under minors or emerging markets on another.

---

Exotic pairs usually see less overall trading activity than major pairs [@babypips-pipsology].

Lower trading activity means fewer buyers and sellers are active at any given moment. This reduced market activity often leads to a wider [[spread]], which increases transaction costs.

---

Lower trading activity can also make prices move in larger, sudden steps.

When fewer orders fill the order book, even modest trade sizes can push market prices across multiple price levels quickly.
