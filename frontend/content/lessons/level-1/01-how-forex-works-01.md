When you trade forex you're exchanging one currency for another. Prices always come as a [[currency pair]], like EUR/USD.

The first currency is the [[base currency]] and the second is the [[quote currency]]. **EUR/USD at 1.0850 means one euro costs 1.0850 US dollars.**

---

Buying EUR/USD means you expect the euro to rise against the dollar. Selling means you expect it to fall. You can do either at any time.

Most retail traders never hold the real currencies. They trade a contract with a [[broker]] that pays or charges the difference in price.

---

::question{type="mc"}
EUR/USD moves from 1.0850 to 1.0900. What happened?
- [ ] The euro got cheaper against the dollar
- [x] The euro got more expensive against the dollar
- [ ] Nothing changed for someone holding euros
? The price is how many dollars one euro costs. It went up, so each euro now costs more dollars.
::

---

::question{type="mc"}
You sold EUR/USD at 1.0900 and the price rose to 1.0950. Did the trade gain or lose?
- [ ] It gained
- [x] It lost
? Selling gains when the price falls. The price rose, so the trade lost.
::

---

::takeaway
A pair's price is how much of the second currency one unit of the first costs. Buying gains when it rises; selling gains when it falls.
::
