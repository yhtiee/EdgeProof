Not every hour is a good hour to trade. Around 5 p.m. New York time, the trading day ends and a new one begins. This is the daily rollover, when [[swap|swaps]] are charged.

For a short time around the rollover, many banks step back. [[liquidity]] thins out, and spreads can widen to several times their normal size.

---

Wide spreads cause trouble that has nothing to do with your idea. A [[stop-loss]] that's close to the price can be hit just because the spread widened for a few minutes, even if the chart barely moved.

The same can happen late on Friday, early on Sunday after the weekend open, and on public holidays.

---

? Around the rollover, spreads can widen sharply. A sell stop is triggered by the bid and a buy stop by the ask, so a wider spread alone can reach a tight stop.
::

---

There's a simple way to stay out of trouble: decide in advance which hours you trade, and write them into your rules. EdgeProof's rulebook has a place for no-trade windows like this.

::takeaway
Liquidity changes through the day. Around the daily rollover, late Friday and early Sunday, spreads can widen sharply, so many traders keep these hours out of their plans.
::
