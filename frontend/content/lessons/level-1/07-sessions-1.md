Forex has no single building that opens and closes. Instead, trading follows the business day around the world: it starts in Asia, moves to Europe, then to North America.

Each of these busy periods is called a [[session]]. Traders usually talk about three: Asia (with Tokyo, Hong Kong and Singapore), London and New York.

---

London is the biggest centre of all. The UK remained the largest centre of foreign exchange trading in April 2025, with 37.8% of global turnover [@boe-uk-fx-survey-2025].

When two sessions are open at once, more banks and traders are active. The London and New York overlap is usually the busiest few hours of the day.

Busy hours matter to you in two ways. Prices tend to move more, which some strategies need. And there are more buyers and sellers, so the [[spread]] is often narrower.

Quiet hours are the opposite: smaller moves, and sometimes wider spreads.

Forex follows the sun from Asia to London to New York. The overlaps, especially London with New York, are usually the busiest hours, with the most movement and the tightest spreads.
