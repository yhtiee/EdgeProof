Where there's money and hope, there are scams. Forex attracts more than most, because the results are hard for beginners to check.

The good news: most scams use the same few lines. Once you know them, they're easy to spot.

---

**Red flags to watch for:**

- Promises of fixed or guaranteed returns, like "10% a month".
- "Account managers" who ask for your login or your money to trade for you.
- Signal groups selling trade calls, with screenshots of wins and no full record.
- Pressure: limited spots, countdowns, "join today or miss out".
- Requests to pay in crypto, to a personal account, or to recruit friends.

---

Big monthly promises sound small. They aren't. Set the claim to 10% a month and see what that would mean over a few years.

---

10% a month compounds to more than 200% a year. Anyone who could really do that wouldn't need your money: they could turn a small account into a fortune on their own in a few years. When someone sells it to you instead, the product is you.

---

::question{type="mc"}
Someone offers to trade your account for you and promises 8% a month, every month. What's the most likely explanation?
- [ ] They've found a reliable strategy and want to share it
- [x] It's a scam: real trading results are never fixed or guaranteed
- [ ] It's normal for professional managers to promise this
? No real trading produces a fixed monthly return. Guarantees and account managers who want your money or login are classic signs of a scam.
::

---

::takeaway
Guaranteed returns, account managers, paid signals and pressure to act fast are red flags. Real trading results go up and down, and no honest person can promise them.
::
