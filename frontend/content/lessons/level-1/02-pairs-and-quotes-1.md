Every exchange rate is written as a [[currency pair]], showing two currencies side by side [@babypips-pipsology].

The first currency listed in the pair is called the [[base currency]]. The second currency listed is called the [[quote currency]].

---

The price of a pair shows how much of the quote currency is needed to buy 1 unit of the base currency [@babypips-pipsology].

In the EUR/USD pair, the euro is the base currency and the US dollar is the quote currency.

---

If EUR/USD is quoted at 1.0850, buying 1 euro costs 1.0850 US dollars.

If the rate moves to 1.0860, 1 euro now costs 1.0860 US dollars. The base currency has gained value relative to the quote currency.

---

::question{type="mc"}
In the GBP/USD pair, which currency is the quote currency?
- [ ] GBP (British pound)
- [x] USD (US dollar)
? The quote currency is always the second currency listed in a pair. In GBP/USD, USD is second, so it is the quote currency.
::

---

::question{type="mc"}
If USD/JPY is quoted at 150.00, what does this price represent?
- [x] 1 US dollar costs 150.00 yen
- [ ] 1 yen costs 150.00 US dollars
? USD is the base currency and JPY is the quote currency. The price shows that 1 unit of the base currency (1 dollar) requires 150.00 units of the quote currency (150.00 yen).
::

---

::takeaway
An exchange rate compares two currencies: the base currency comes first, and the price shows how much quote currency is required to buy 1 unit of it.
::
