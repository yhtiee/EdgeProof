A rate decision day has three parts, and each can move the market:

1. **The decision:** raise, cut or hold, and by how much.
2. **The statement:** a short text explaining why, and hinting at what comes next.
3. **The press conference or minutes:** more detail, and answers to questions.

---

Traders compare all three with what was expected. A hold that was expected can still move the currency a lot if the statement hints at a rate rise sooner than people thought.

Central banks publish their decisions and statements on their own websites, such as the European Central Bank's monetary policy pages [@ecb-monetary-policy].

---

For your trading, the practical point is timing. Prices can swing sharply in both directions within minutes, spreads widen, and stops can slip. Most strategies are tested on normal market conditions, not these moments.

---

::question{type="mc"}
A central bank holds rates as expected, but its statement says rates may rise sooner than markets thought. What's the likely effect on its currency?
- [ ] No effect, because the rate didn't change
- [x] The currency may rise, because expectations of higher rates increased
- [ ] The currency must fall
? Markets react to the outlook as much as the decision. A hint of earlier rate rises makes the currency more attractive.
::

---

::takeaway
A rate decision is the decision, the statement and the press conference, all judged against expectations. The outlook can move prices more than the decision itself.
::
