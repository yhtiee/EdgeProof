Currencies don't move in isolation. They're linked to stocks, bonds and commodities, because the same big investors trade all of them. Studying those links is called intermarket analysis [@murphy-technical-analysis].

---

Traders often describe the mood of markets as **risk-on** or **risk-off**:

- **Risk-on:** investors feel confident. Money tends to flow into stocks and higher-yielding currencies.
- **Risk-off:** investors are worried. Money tends to flow toward assets seen as safer.

The Japanese yen, the Swiss franc and the US dollar have often been treated as safe havens in worried markets, though this has varied over time.

---

Commodities matter for some currencies too. Countries that export a lot of one commodity can see their currency move with its price. The Australian dollar is often linked to metals, and the Canadian dollar and Norwegian krone to oil.

These links are tendencies, not rules. They strengthen, weaken and sometimes reverse.

::takeaway
Currencies move with the wider market mood and, for some, with commodity prices. These links are useful context but they change over time, so treat them as tendencies.
::
