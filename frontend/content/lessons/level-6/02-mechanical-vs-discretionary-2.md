If you want some judgement in your strategy, you can still keep it honest. The trick is to limit where judgement is allowed, and record every time you use it.

---

**Ways to box in discretion:**

- Make the setup mechanical, and allow judgement only to **skip** a trade, never to add one.
- Give skipping a list of allowed reasons, such as "major news within 1 hour".
- Log every skipped setup with its reason, so you can later check whether skipping helped.

---

That last step is powerful. If your skipped setups would have done better than the ones you took, your judgement is costing you, and the data will show it.

---

::question{type="mc"}
Which use of discretion is easiest to keep honest?
- [ ] Taking extra trades when a chart feels promising
- [x] Skipping valid setups only for listed reasons, and logging each skip
- [ ] Changing the stop on each trade depending on mood
? Skipping for listed, logged reasons can be checked later. Adding trades or changing stops by feel can't.
::

---

::takeaway
If you use judgement, limit it to skipping trades for listed reasons and log every skip. Then your data can show whether your judgement helps or hurts.
::
