A good test for any rule: could a stranger follow it on a chart and get exactly your answer? If not, it can't be tested. David Aronson makes this point about trading rules: only objective rules can be tested scientifically [@aronson-ebta].

---

Rules break the test in a few common ways. Words like "strong", "clear" or "looks like" mean something different to everyone, and to you on different days. EdgeProof's clarity checks flag them.

Instead, write each condition in a structured way: **what** you measure, **how** you compare it, **what value**, and on **which timeframe**.

---

Compare:

- Vague: "Price is in a strong uptrend."
- Structured: "Daily close is above the 50-day simple moving average, and the average is higher than 10 days ago."

The second can be checked by anyone, on any chart, in seconds.

---

Each rule also needs two example screenshots: one where it's met, and one where it nearly is but isn't. The invalid example is the important one: it shows exactly where the line is.

---

::question{type="mc"}
Which rule would pass EdgeProof's clarity checks?
- [ ] "Enter when there's clear rejection at support"
- [ ] "Enter when the candle looks like a strong pin bar"
- [x] "Enter when a 1-hour candle closes back above the support zone, with a lower wick at least twice its body"
? The last one is measurable. "Clear", "strong" and "looks like" are on the vague-words list for good reason.
::

---

::takeaway
Write every rule so a stranger would get your answer: what, compared how, against what value, on which timeframe. Add a valid and an invalid example for each one.
::
