Everything so far leads here. An idea like "buy pullbacks in an uptrend" isn't a strategy yet. A strategy is a complete set of rules that tells you exactly what to do, every time.

---

A full rulebook answers every question a trade raises:

- **Market:** which pairs, which [[timeframe|timeframes]], which [[session|sessions]].
- **Setup:** the conditions that must all be true.
- **Entry:** what triggers the trade, and the order type.
- **Stop:** where the initial [[stop-loss]] goes. Every trade needs one.
- **Exit:** the target, and any trade management.

---

- **Risk:** risk per trade, daily and weekly loss limits, maximum [[drawdown]], maximum trades.
- **Costs:** spread, commission, slippage and swap for each pair.
- **No-trade rules:** news windows, spread limits, other conditions.
- **Examples:** a screenshot of a valid setup and an invalid one, for each rule.

EdgeProof's Strategy Lab walks you through these parts one at a time.

::question{type="mc"}
A trader's plan says: "Buy pullbacks in an uptrend on EUR/USD." What's the biggest gap?
- [ ] It doesn't name a pair
- [x] It doesn't define the trend, the pullback, the entry, the stop, the exit or the risk
- [ ] It's too short to read
? It's an idea, not a rulebook. Two traders could follow it and take completely different trades.
::

---

::takeaway
A strategy is a full rulebook: market, setup, entry, stop, exit, risk, costs, no-trade rules and examples. Until every part is written, you have an idea, not something you can test.
::
