A [[trading journal]] is a record of every trade: what you did, why, and how it turned out. Without one, you're relying on memory, and memory keeps the dramatic trades and forgets the ordinary ones.

---

**For each trade, record the facts:**

- Pair, direction, entry, initial [[stop-loss]], exit and size.
- The result in money and in R.
- The setup it was, and a screenshot before and after.

**And the things that explain the facts:**

- Did you follow every rule? Which ones did you break?
- How did you feel before and after, and how confident were you?
- Anything else going on: tiredness, distractions, a loss just before.

---

The second list is what makes a journal useful. Facts tell you what happened; the context tells you why. After 30 or more trades, patterns appear, such as losing trades bunching up when you trade tired.

::takeaway
Record every trade: the facts, the rules you followed or broke, and how you felt. The context is what turns a list of trades into something you can learn from.
