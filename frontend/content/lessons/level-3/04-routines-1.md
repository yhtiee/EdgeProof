Pilots run a checklist before every flight, however experienced they are. Not because they forget how to fly, but because a checklist catches the things a busy mind skips.

Trading psychologist Brett Steenbarger describes routines like this as a core part of self-coaching: small, repeated habits that keep you trading your plan [@steenbarger-daily-trading-coach].

---

**A short pre-session checklist might cover:**

- **News:** any big releases today? Mark the no-trade windows.
- **Sessions:** which hours am I trading, and is now one of them?
- **State:** have I slept? Am I calm, or rushed, angry or distracted?
- **Limits:** what's today's loss limit in R, and how many trades at most?
- **Never-again list:** read it once.

---

The "state" check matters more than it looks. Tiredness and stress make every trap from this level more likely. Some traders skip the session entirely on a bad day, and record that as a good decision.

---

::question{type="mc"}
You slept three hours and you're upset about something outside trading. What fits a pre-session routine?
- [ ] Trade as normal; feelings don't affect a good strategy
- [x] Note your state, and consider trading smaller or not at all today
- [ ] Trade more to take your mind off it
? Your state affects whether you follow your rules. Noticing it, and adjusting, is the point of the check.
::

---

::takeaway
A short checklist before each session catches news, timing, your own state and your limits. It takes two minutes and prevents many unforced mistakes.
::
