After a loss, there's a strong pull to win the money back straight away. Acting on that pull is called revenge trading. Traders also call the state behind it being "on [[tilt]]".

It's one of the fastest ways to turn a normal losing day into a terrible one.

---

Revenge trades look different from planned ones:

- They come soon after a loss, often within minutes.
- They're bigger than usual, to "make it back".
- They're taken without a valid setup, or with the rules bent.
- One leads to another, because each loss raises the pressure.

---

Loss aversion is behind it: a realised loss hurts, and a quick win would make the pain go away [@kahneman-tversky-prospect-theory]. The market doesn't know you lost, though. Your next trade has the same odds as any other.

---

EdgeProof watches for this pattern without blocking you. A trade is tagged as a tilt-window trade when it opens within 30 minutes of two losses in a row, after your daily loss limit is reached, or at more than 1.25 times your planned size. Over time, you'll see what those trades cost you in R.

::takeaway
Revenge trading is trading to win back a loss: fast, oversized and outside the rules. Recognising the signs is the first step to stopping it.
::
