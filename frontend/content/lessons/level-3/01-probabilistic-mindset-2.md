Most trading pain comes from one gap: you enter a trade hoping it wins, without really accepting that it might lose.

When the price moves against you, the loss feels like a surprise. Surprise leads to bad decisions: moving the stop, closing early, or adding more to "fix" it.

---

The cure is simple to say: decide the loss before you enter, and accept it then.

A useful test before every trade: can you say, "If this hits my [[stop-loss]], I lose 1R, which is this many dollars, and that's fine"? If that sentence feels uncomfortable, the trade is too big.

---

This is why EdgeProof makes an initial stop compulsory on every trade, and why the stop can't be edited after you save it. The risk is fixed at the moment you can still think calmly.

---

::question{type="mc"}
A trade moves against you and is close to your stop. Which action fits accepting the risk in advance?
- [ ] Move the stop further away to give it room
- [ ] Close it now to avoid the full loss, then re-enter later
- [x] Leave the stop where your rules put it and let the trade play out
? The risk was decided and accepted before entry. Changing it under pressure turns a planned 1R loss into an unplanned, often bigger, one.
::

---

::question{type="mc"}
Before entering, you notice you feel anxious about losing this trade. What does that suggest?
- [ ] The setup is probably a bad one
- [x] The risk may be bigger than you've truly accepted, so the size may be too large
- [ ] You should wait for a setup that feels certain
? No setup is certain. Anxiety about a normal loss usually means the money at risk is more than you're comfortable losing.
::

---

::takeaway
Decide and accept the loss before you enter. If losing 1R on this trade feels unacceptable, reduce the size until it doesn't.
::
