A pair has just moved 80 pips without you. It feels like the move of the month, and you're watching it leave. The urge to jump in now, at any price, is called fear of missing out.

---

Chasing has a hidden cost. Entering late usually means your entry is far from where a sensible [[stop-loss]] would go. So either the stop is very wide, which forces a tiny size, or it's too tight and gets hit by normal movement.

Either way, the trade no longer matches your tested rules, so your tested [[edge]] doesn't apply to it.

---

Greed shows up the other way too: adding size after a few wins, because you feel you can't lose. That's the moment your risk is highest and your judgement weakest.

---

::question{type="mc"}
You missed your planned entry, and the price has run 60 pips. Your rules don't have a setup here. What does your plan say?
- [ ] Enter now with a small stop to catch the rest of the move
- [x] Let it go: there's no valid setup, and another one will come
- [ ] Enter with double size to make up for missing the start
? A trade outside your rules isn't part of your tested edge. Missing a move costs nothing; chasing one can cost a lot.
::

---

::takeaway
Missing a move costs nothing. Chasing it, or sizing up after wins, puts you outside the rules your edge was tested on.
::
