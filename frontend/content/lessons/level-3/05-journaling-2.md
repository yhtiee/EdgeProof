A journal pays off when you look back at it with questions. The most useful question is: what does breaking my rules cost me?

---

Split your trades into two groups: those where you followed every rule, and those where you broke at least one. Then compare their results in R.

If the rule-breaking group is where most of the losses are, you've found a number to work on that's fully in your control.

---

::question{type="numeric" answer="6" tolerance="0" unit="R"}
Over a month, your rule-following trades totalled +4R and your rule-breaking trades totalled −6R. How many R did breaking your rules cost you? Write it as a positive number.
? The rule-breaking trades lost 6R. Without them, the month would have been +4R instead of −2R.
::

---

Other useful comparisons:

- Results by [[session]]: do you trade better at certain hours?
- Results by setup: is one setup carrying the others?
- Results by mood or sleep: do certain states lead to losses?

EdgeProof shows these breakdowns for you as your journal grows.

---

Keep the [[sample size]] in mind. A pattern across 8 trades could be luck; the same pattern across 80 is worth acting on.

::takeaway
Compare your rule-following and rule-breaking trades: the gap is often the biggest improvement available. Look for patterns, but wait for enough trades before trusting one.
::
