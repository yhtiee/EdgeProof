Compounding means earning returns on your returns. If an account grows 10% this month, next month's 10% is calculated on the bigger balance.

Over long periods, compounding turns small, steady numbers into very large ones. That's exactly why it's used to sell dreams.

---

Set the monthly return to 5%, then 10%, then 20%, and watch what happens over a few years.

::simulator{kind="compounding"}

---

::question{type="numeric" answer="214" tolerance="2" unit="%"}
An advert promises 10% a month. If that compounded for 12 months, what would the yearly return be, in percent? Round to the nearest whole number.
? 1.10¹² ≈ 3.14, so the account would be about 3.14 times its start: a gain of about 214% in a year.
::

---

Returns like that, kept up for ten years, would turn $1,000 into tens of millions of dollars. Real results aren't smooth, they come with drawdowns, and big monthly numbers usually come from big risk or from luck over a short period.

::takeaway
Compounding makes monthly claims sound modest when they're extraordinary. Turn any monthly promise into a yearly number before you believe it.
::
