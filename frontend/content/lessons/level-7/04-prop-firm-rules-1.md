A [[prop firm]] sells you a challenge: pay a fee, trade a demo account under its rules, and if you pass, it offers you a "funded" account and a share of any profits.

This lesson explains the rules so you can judge them against your own data. EdgeProof doesn't rank firms or recommend any.

---

**Rules most challenges use:**

- **Profit target:** for example, +8% or +10% to pass.
- **Daily loss limit:** for example, no more than 5% lost in one day.
- **Maximum drawdown:** for example, never more than 10% below the starting balance. Some firms measure it from the highest balance reached, which trails upward as you profit.
- **Time and day rules:** minimum trading days, sometimes a deadline.
- **Other rules:** consistency rules, news-trading bans, limits on holding over weekends.

---

Read the small print on how drawdown is measured. Some firms count only closed trades; others count open, floating losses in real time. A trade that dips deep before recovering can break the rule on a floating basis even if it closes in profit.

---

::question{type="numeric" answer="5" tolerance="0" unit="losses"}
A challenge has a 5% daily loss limit. You risk 1% per trade. How many full losses in a row in one day would hit the limit?
? 5% ÷ 1% = 5 losses. Losing streaks of that length are normal for many strategies, so check how often your data shows them in a single day.
::

---

::takeaway
Challenges combine a profit target with daily and overall loss limits, plus extra rules. How drawdown is measured, closed or floating, fixed or trailing, can matter as much as the numbers.
::
