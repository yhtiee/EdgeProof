Once you're trading a tested strategy, profit alone tells you very little. Here are the numbers that do, and the question each one answers:

- **[[expectancy|Expectancy]], with its range:** is the edge still there?
- **[[profit factor|Profit factor]]:** do the wins still outweigh the losses comfortably?
- **Maximum drawdown, against your plan:** is the pain within what you planned for?

---

- **Rule-following rate:** what share of trades followed every rule?
- **Costs as a share of results:** are spreads and slippage worse than your cost model?
- **Sample size:** how many trades are these numbers based on?

Jack Schwager's interviews with successful traders return again and again to the same theme: control risk first [@schwager-market-wizards].

---

Read the numbers together. A falling expectancy with a high rule-following rate points to the market or the strategy. A falling expectancy with a low rule-following rate points to you. They lead to very different fixes.

---

::question{type="mc"}
Over 60 trades, expectancy has dropped, and the rule-following rate is 72%. What should be looked at first?
- [ ] Whether the strategy has stopped working
- [x] The rule-breaking trades, because more than a quarter of trades broke the rules
- [ ] Switching to a new strategy
? With 28% of trades breaking rules, the results aren't really the strategy's. Fix discipline first; only then can the data judge the strategy.
::

---

::takeaway
Track expectancy with its range, profit factor, drawdown against plan, rule-following, costs and sample size. Read them together to tell a discipline problem from a strategy problem.
::
