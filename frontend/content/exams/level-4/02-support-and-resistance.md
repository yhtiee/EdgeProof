::question{id="sr-1" lesson="l4-support-and-resistance-zones"}
Support is:
- [x] A price area where falling prices have repeatedly stopped and turned up
- [ ] A broker's help desk
- [ ] A level price can never go below
? Support is an area where buyers have stepped in before, not a guaranteed floor.
::
::question{id="sr-2" lesson="l4-support-and-resistance-zones"}
Why treat support and resistance as zones rather than exact lines?
- [x] Price rarely turns at exactly the same price each time
- [ ] Charts can't show exact prices
- [ ] Zones are required by regulators
? Turns cluster around an area, so a zone fits the evidence better.
::
::question{id="sr-3" lesson="l4-support-and-resistance-zones"}
Price has turned down near 1.2500 three times. That area is:
- [ ] Support
- [x] Resistance
- [ ] A trend line
? Resistance is where rising prices have repeatedly stalled.
::
::question{id="sr-4" lesson="l4-support-and-resistance-zones"}
Which statement is accurate?
- [ ] A support level that held three times will hold the fourth time
- [x] A level that held before shows where buyers acted, but it can still break
- [ ] Support only exists on daily charts
? Past reactions shift the odds, they don't guarantee the next one.
::
::question{id="sr-5" lesson="l4-role-reversal-and-false-breaks"}
Role reversal means:
- [x] Broken resistance often acts as support later, and broken support as resistance
- [ ] Buyers and sellers swap brokers
- [ ] The chart flips upside down
? Former ceilings often become floors after a break, and the other way round.
::
::question{id="sr-6" lesson="l4-role-reversal-and-false-breaks"}
Price pushes above resistance, then quickly falls back below it. This is called:
- [x] A false break (fakeout)
- [ ] Role reversal
- [ ] A gap
? False breaks are common, which is why breakout rules need testing.
::
::question{id="sr-7" lesson="l4-role-reversal-and-false-breaks"}
How can you make a breakout rule testable?
- [x] Define exactly what counts as a break, such as a candle close beyond the zone
- [ ] Trade whenever it "looks like" a breakout
- [ ] Only trade breakouts you feel good about
? A precise definition lets you count how often breaks follow through.
::
::question{id="sr-8" lesson="l4-role-reversal-and-false-breaks"}
Why might you see many false breaks around obvious levels?
- [x] Many traders place orders there, so price often pokes through before reversing
- [ ] Levels are fake
- [ ] Brokers move price to hit stops on every level
? Clustered stops and orders at obvious levels create quick spikes through them.
::
