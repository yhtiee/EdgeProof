::question{id="tr-1" lesson="l4-defining-a-trend"}
Which trend definition can be tested?
- [x] "The trend is up when the daily close is above the 50-day simple moving average"
- [ ] "The trend is up when it feels strong"
- [ ] "The trend is up when people online are bullish"
? A testable definition is precise enough that anyone would get the same answer.
::
::question{id="tr-2" lesson="l4-defining-a-trend"}
By structure, a downtrend is:
- [x] Lower highs and lower lows
- [ ] Higher highs and higher lows
- [ ] Price moving sideways
? Falling peaks and falling valleys describe a downtrend.
::
::question{id="tr-3" lesson="l4-defining-a-trend"}
Two traders look at the same chart. One says "uptrend", the other says "range". What does this show?
- [x] The trend needs a precise definition, or it means different things to different people
- [ ] One of them is cheating
- [ ] Charts can't show trends
? Clear rules remove the argument.
::
::question{id="tr-4" lesson="l4-defining-a-trend"}
Which is a common way to define a trend?
- [x] By structure (highs and lows) or by a moving average
- [ ] By the colour of the last candle only
- [ ] By the day of the week
? Both methods turn "trend" into something you can measure.
::
::question{id="tr-5" lesson="l4-trends-and-ranges"}
A range is:
- [x] Price moving sideways between a rough ceiling and floor
- [ ] A strong one-way move
- [ ] A type of order
? In a range, neither buyers nor sellers are in control for now.
::
::question{id="tr-6" lesson="l4-trends-and-ranges"}
Why does it matter whether the market is trending or ranging?
- [x] Strategies that work in trends often lose in ranges, and the other way round
- [ ] Brokers charge more in ranges
- [ ] It doesn't matter at all
? Matching the method to the market's state is part of a strategy's rules.
::
::question{id="tr-7" lesson="l4-trends-and-ranges"}
A trend-following strategy is most likely to have a losing stretch when:
- [ ] The market trends strongly
- [x] The market chops sideways in a range
- [ ] The spread is low
? Sideways markets create many false starts for trend followers.
::
::question{id="tr-8" lesson="l4-trends-and-ranges"}
Markets often switch between trends and ranges. What does that mean for a strategy?
- [x] Expect stretches where it doesn't suit the market, and size for them
- [ ] Change strategy every day
- [ ] Only trade when you're certain of the trend
? No strategy fits every market state, so losing stretches are expected.
::
