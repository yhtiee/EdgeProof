::question{id="im-1" lesson="l5-risk-on-and-risk-off"}
"Risk-off" describes a market mood where investors are:
- [x] Worried, and move money toward safer assets
- [ ] Confident, and buy riskier assets
- [ ] On holiday
? In risk-off, money tends to flow to perceived safe havens.
::
::question{id="im-2" lesson="l5-risk-on-and-risk-off"}
Which currency has often been treated as a safe haven?
- [x] The Japanese yen
- [ ] The Australian dollar
- [ ] The Turkish lira
? The yen, Swiss franc and US dollar have often been sought in worried markets.
::
::question{id="im-3" lesson="l5-risk-on-and-risk-off"}
In a risk-on mood, which currencies have often done well?
- [x] Higher-yielding and commodity-linked currencies, such as the Australian dollar
- [ ] Only safe havens
- [ ] None of them
? When investors feel confident, they reach for higher returns.
::
::question{id="im-4" lesson="l5-risk-on-and-risk-off"}
A country exports a lot of one commodity. When that commodity's price falls sharply, its currency often:
- [x] Weakens
- [ ] Strengthens
- [ ] Is unaffected
? Lower export income tends to weigh on the currency.
::
::question{id="im-5" lesson="l5-pairs-that-move-together"}
EUR/USD and GBP/USD often move in the same direction because:
- [x] Both have the US dollar as the quote currency, so dollar moves affect both
- [ ] They are the same pair
- [ ] Brokers link them on purpose
? A shared currency creates a shared driver.
::
::question{id="im-6" lesson="l5-pairs-that-move-together"}
You buy EUR/USD and GBP/USD at 1% risk each. Your real risk to a broad US dollar rally is:
- [x] Closer to 2%, because both trades lose if the dollar rises
- [ ] 1%, because they are different pairs
- [ ] Zero, because they cancel out
? Correlated trades stack risk on the same driver.
::
::question{id="im-7" lesson="l5-pairs-that-move-together"}
A correlation of +0.9 between two pairs means:
- [x] They have tended to move in the same direction most of the time
- [ ] They always move by exactly the same amount
- [ ] They move in opposite directions
? Correlation measures how closely they've moved together, and it can change over time.
::
::question{id="im-8" lesson="l5-pairs-that-move-together"}
Why shouldn't you assume a correlation will last?
- [x] Relationships between markets shift as conditions change
- [ ] Correlations are fixed by law
- [ ] Correlations only exist on weekends
? Past correlation is a guide, not a guarantee.
::
