::question{id="cb-1" lesson="l5-what-central-banks-do"}
A central bank's main tool for steering the economy is:
- [x] Setting its key interest rate
- [ ] Choosing the exchange rate every morning
- [ ] Setting the price of stocks
? By raising or lowering its policy rate, it influences borrowing, spending and inflation.
::
::question{id="cb-2" lesson="l5-what-central-banks-do"}
Which is a central bank?
- [x] The US Federal Reserve
- [ ] The International Monetary Fund
- [ ] A large commercial bank such as a retail high-street bank
? The Fed, the European Central Bank, the Bank of England and the Bank of Japan are central banks.
::
::question{id="cb-3" lesson="l5-what-central-banks-do"}
When inflation runs well above its target, a central bank will usually:
- [x] Raise rates, or signal that it may
- [ ] Cut rates sharply
- [ ] Print banknotes in a new colour
? Higher rates cool demand and help bring inflation down.
::
::question{id="cb-4" lesson="l5-what-central-banks-do"}
Why do central bank speeches and signals move currencies even without a rate change?
- [x] They change what markets expect the bank to do next
- [ ] Speeches are legally binding
- [ ] Brokers adjust spreads during speeches
? Guidance about the future shifts expectations, and expectations move prices.
::
::question{id="cb-5" lesson="l5-reading-a-rate-decision"}
A central bank holds rates as expected but says future rises are more likely than before. The currency will most likely:
- [x] Rise, because the outlook for rates has shifted up
- [ ] Fall, because rates didn't change
- [ ] Not move at all
? The change in outlook is the surprise, even with no change in the rate.
::
::question{id="cb-6" lesson="l5-reading-a-rate-decision"}
"Hawkish" describes a central bank that leans toward:
- [x] Higher rates to fight inflation
- [ ] Lower rates to support growth
- [ ] Fixing the exchange rate
? Hawkish leans tighter; dovish leans looser.
::
::question{id="cb-7" lesson="l5-reading-a-rate-decision"}
A central bank cuts rates by 0.25% when markets expected 0.50%. The currency will most likely:
- [x] Rise, because the cut was smaller than expected
- [ ] Fall sharply, because any cut is bad
- [ ] Stay flat
? Relative to expectations, a smaller cut is a hawkish surprise.
::
::question{id="cb-8" lesson="l5-reading-a-rate-decision"}
Right after a rate decision, why can price swing both ways within minutes?
- [x] Traders digest the decision, the statement and the press conference, each of which can surprise
- [ ] The decision is announced twice
- [ ] Brokers close the market
? Mixed signals arrive in stages, so early moves often reverse.
::
