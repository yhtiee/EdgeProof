::question{id="ps-1" lesson="l2-sizing-from-the-stop"}
In good position sizing, which do you decide first?
- [x] Where your stop goes, based on the chart and the idea
- [ ] The lot size you'd like to trade
- [ ] How much profit you want
? Place the stop where the idea is wrong, then size the position so that distance costs your chosen risk.
::
::question{id="ps-2" type="numeric" answer="20" tolerance="0" unit="$" lesson="l2-how-much-to-risk-per-trade"}
Your account is $2,000 and you risk 1% per trade. How much money is at risk on each trade?
? 1% of $2,000 = $20.
::
::question{id="ps-3" type="numeric" answer="1" tolerance="0" unit="$ per pip" lesson="l2-sizing-from-the-stop"}
You will risk $20 and your stop is 20 pips away. What pip value should your position have, in dollars per pip?
? Pip value = money at risk ÷ stop distance = $20 ÷ 20 = $1 per pip.
::
::question{id="ps-4" type="numeric" answer="0.1" tolerance="0" unit="lots" lesson="l2-sizing-from-the-stop"}
On EUR/USD, one standard lot is worth $10 per pip. You need $1 per pip. How many lots should you trade?
? $1 ÷ $10 = 0.1 lots (a mini lot, 10,000 units).
::
::question{id="ps-5" lesson="l2-sizing-from-the-stop"}
Your stop needs to be wider than usual. To keep the same risk you should:
- [x] Trade a smaller position
- [ ] Trade a bigger position
- [ ] Remove the stop
? A wider stop with the same money at risk means fewer units.
::
::question{id="ps-6" lesson="l2-how-much-to-risk-per-trade"}
A common guideline for how much to risk per trade, especially while learning, is:
- [x] Around 0.5% to 1% of the account
- [ ] 10% of the account
- [ ] Whatever the broker's maximum leverage allows
? Small risk keeps losing streaks survivable while you prove your edge.
::
::question{id="ps-7" lesson="l2-how-much-to-risk-per-trade"}
After 10 losses in a row at 2% risk per trade, roughly how much of the account is left?
- [ ] About 50%
- [x] About 82%
- [ ] About 98%
? 0.98 multiplied ten times is about 0.82, so roughly 82% is left (about 18% down).
::
::question{id="ps-8" lesson="l2-how-much-to-risk-per-trade"}
Why risk a percentage of the current account rather than a fixed dollar amount forever?
- [ ] It makes every trade a winner
- [x] Risk shrinks automatically after losses and grows after gains
- [ ] It removes the need for a stop
? Percentage risk slows drawdowns and scales up with success.
::
