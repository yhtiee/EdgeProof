::question{id="rr-1" lesson="l2-compounding-and-big-claims"}
Someone claims 20% a month every month. Compounded for a year, that would turn $1,000 into about:
- [ ] $3,400
- [x] $8,900
- [ ] $1,240
? 1.2 to the power of 12 is about 8.9. Claims like this are almost never sustained.
::
::question{id="rr-2" lesson="l2-compounding-and-big-claims"}
Why are claims of huge, steady monthly returns a warning sign?
- [x] Even the best professional funds rarely sustain anything close to them
- [ ] They are illegal to post online
- [ ] Monthly returns can't be measured
? Real long-term returns, even at top funds, are far lower than most online claims.
::
::question{id="rr-3" lesson="l2-compounding-and-big-claims"}
Compounding means:
- [x] Gains are added to the account, so later gains are made on a bigger base
- [ ] Taking all profits out each month
- [ ] Trading two pairs at once
? Growth builds on growth, which is powerful but slow at realistic rates.
::
::question{id="rr-4" type="numeric" answer="21" tolerance="0.2" unit="%" lesson="l2-compounding-and-big-claims"}
An account gains 10% in each of two years, compounded. What is the total gain, in %?
? 1.1 × 1.1 = 1.21, so the total gain is 21%.
::
::question{id="rr-5" lesson="l2-what-an-edge-is-worth"}
A strategy makes 0.2R per trade on average and you risk 1% per trade. Over 100 trades, roughly what is the expected gain before compounding?
- [x] About 20%
- [ ] About 2%
- [ ] About 200%
? 0.2R × 100 trades = 20R, and at 1% per R that is about 20%.
::
::question{id="rr-6" lesson="l2-what-an-edge-is-worth"}
What decides how much an edge is worth in money?
- [x] The size of the edge, how often it trades and how much you risk per trade
- [ ] Only the win rate
- [ ] Only the account size
? Expectancy × number of trades × risk per trade sets the expected return.
::
::question{id="rr-7" lesson="l2-what-an-edge-is-worth"}
Why can't you simply raise risk per trade to earn more from a small edge?
- [x] Higher risk also deepens drawdowns and raises the chance of ruin
- [ ] Brokers forbid it
- [ ] Higher risk lowers the win rate
? Return and risk scale together. Too much risk can end the account before the edge pays.
::
::question{id="rr-8" lesson="l2-what-an-edge-is-worth"}
Which expectation is the most realistic for a beginner's first year?
- [ ] Doubling the account every month
- [x] Learning, keeping losses small and building evidence about an edge
- [ ] Replacing a full-time salary right away
? The first goal is survival and evidence, not big returns.
::
