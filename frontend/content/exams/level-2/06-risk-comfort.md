::question{id="rc-1" lesson="l2-what-drawdowns-feel-like"}
Why do drawdowns feel worse in real life than on paper?
- [x] Real money and long losing stretches create stress that leads to bad decisions
- [ ] Brokers make losses bigger on live accounts
- [ ] Paper accounts don't have losing trades
? Emotions under real losses push people to break their rules.
::
::question{id="rc-2" lesson="l2-what-drawdowns-feel-like"}
A backtest shows a worst drawdown of 15%. What should you expect live?
- [ ] Never more than 15%
- [x] That a deeper drawdown is possible, because the future can be worse than the test
- [ ] No drawdowns at all
? A backtest shows one past path. Plan for worse.
::
::question{id="rc-3" lesson="l2-what-drawdowns-feel-like"}
During a drawdown, which reaction is most dangerous?
- [ ] Reviewing whether you followed your rules
- [x] Increasing size to win back losses quickly
- [ ] Taking a short break
? Bigger size in a drawdown deepens it and risks ruin.
::
::question{id="rc-4" lesson="l2-what-drawdowns-feel-like"}
Losing streaks and drawdowns are:
- [x] A normal part of any strategy, even a good one
- [ ] A sign the strategy is broken
- [ ] Avoidable with the right indicator
? Every real edge has losing stretches. The question is whether they stay within expectations.
::
::question{id="rc-5" lesson="l2-choosing-a-risk-you-can-live-with"}
How should you choose your risk per trade?
- [x] Pick a level where a long losing streak would still be emotionally and financially bearable
- [ ] Pick the highest level your broker allows
- [ ] Copy whatever a famous trader uses
? The right risk is one you can stick with through bad stretches.
::
::question{id="rc-6" lesson="l2-choosing-a-risk-you-can-live-with"}
You notice you can't sleep when trades are open. What is the best adjustment?
- [ ] Trade more often to get used to it
- [x] Reduce your position size
- [ ] Remove stop-losses so you don't see them hit
? Stress is a sign the size is too large for you.
::
::question{id="rc-7" lesson="l2-choosing-a-risk-you-can-live-with"}
Setting a maximum drawdown limit, such as stopping to review after a 10% fall, helps because:
- [x] It forces a pause to check what is wrong before losses grow
- [ ] It guarantees you won't lose more than 10%
- [ ] It makes the strategy profitable
? A preset limit turns a slow slide into a planned review.
::
::question{id="rc-8" lesson="l2-choosing-a-risk-you-can-live-with"}
Which is the best reason to choose a lower risk than you can technically afford?
- [ ] Lower risk always makes more money
- [x] You are more likely to follow your rules through a losing streak
- [ ] Brokers reward low-risk accounts
? A plan you follow beats a "better" plan you abandon halfway through a drawdown.
::
