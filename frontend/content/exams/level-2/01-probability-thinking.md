::question{id="pt-1" lesson="l2-what-probability-means"}
A setup wins 60% of the time. What does that tell you about the next trade?
- [ ] It will win
- [x] It has a 60% chance of winning, and it can still lose
- [ ] It will lose, because the last one won
? Probability describes many trades, not a promise about any single one.
::
::question{id="pt-2" type="numeric" answer="40" tolerance="0" unit="%" lesson="l2-what-probability-means"}
A strategy wins 60% of its trades. What percentage of its trades lose?
? Every trade either wins or loses here, so 100% − 60% = 40%.
::
::question{id="pt-3" lesson="l2-what-probability-means"}
A coin lands heads five times in a row. The chance of heads on the next flip is:
- [ ] Lower, because tails is "due"
- [x] Still 50%
- [ ] Higher, because heads is "hot"
? Each flip is independent. Believing otherwise is the gambler's fallacy.
::
::question{id="pt-4" lesson="l2-what-probability-means"}
Which statement shows good probability thinking?
- [ ] "This trade can't lose"
- [x] "This setup has an edge over many trades, but any single trade is uncertain"
- [ ] "I lost three in a row, so the next one must win"
? Think in odds and outcomes over a series, not certainty on one trade.
::
::question{id="pt-5" lesson="l2-thinking-in-a-series-of-trades"}
Why judge a strategy over a series of trades instead of one?
- [x] A single trade's result is mostly luck; the edge only shows over many trades
- [ ] Single trades are always losers
- [ ] Brokers only report results in groups of 100
? Randomness dominates single results. The average over many trades reveals the edge.
::
::question{id="pt-6" lesson="l2-thinking-in-a-series-of-trades"}
You followed your plan perfectly and the trade lost. That was:
- [x] A good decision with a bad outcome
- [ ] A bad decision, because it lost
- [ ] Proof the plan doesn't work
? Judge decisions by whether you followed a sound process, not by one outcome.
::
::question{id="pt-7" lesson="l2-thinking-in-a-series-of-trades"}
A strategy with a 50% win rate will, over 100 trades, almost certainly have:
- [ ] Perfectly alternating wins and losses
- [x] Some losing streaks of several trades in a row
- [ ] No more than two losses in a row
? Streaks are normal in random sequences. Plan for them.
::
::question{id="pt-8" lesson="l2-thinking-in-a-series-of-trades"}
How should you size trades if you think in a series?
- [ ] Bigger after a loss, to win it back
- [x] The same fixed risk each time, so no single trade matters too much
- [ ] Bigger when you feel confident
? Consistent risk lets the edge play out across the series.
::
