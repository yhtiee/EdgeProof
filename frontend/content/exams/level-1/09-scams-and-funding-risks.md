::question{id="scam-1" lesson="l1-spotting-trading-scams"}
Someone offers "a guaranteed 10% a month" on your trading account. This is:
- [ ] A good deal if they have nice screenshots
- [x] A classic scam sign: real trading returns are never fixed or guaranteed
- [ ] Normal for professional traders
? No real trading produces fixed, guaranteed returns.
::
::question{id="scam-2" lesson="l1-spotting-trading-scams"}
An "account manager" asks for your trading platform password so they can trade for you. What should you do?
- [ ] Share it if they seem friendly
- [x] Refuse; never share login details or let strangers trade your money
- [ ] Share it only for a small account
? Requests for your login or money are a classic scam pattern.
::
::question{id="scam-3" lesson="l1-spotting-trading-scams"}
Which of these is a warning sign of a scam?
- [x] Pressure to deposit quickly before an "offer" ends
- [ ] A clear risk warning on the website
- [ ] Listing on a regulator's public register
? Urgency stops you from checking. Legitimate firms don't need to rush you.
::
::question{id="scam-4" lesson="l1-checking-a-broker"}
What is the most reliable way to check that a broker is regulated?
- [ ] Read the reviews on its own website
- [x] Find it on the regulator's own public register and check the details match
- [ ] Ask in a social media group
? Only the regulator's register is independent. Scam sites often copy real firms' names.
::
::question{id="scam-5" lesson="l1-checking-a-broker"}
A "clone firm" is:
- [ ] A broker with two offices
- [x] A scam that copies a real, regulated firm's name and details
- [ ] A broker that copies other traders' positions
? Check the website, phone number and email against the register, not just the name.
::
::question{id="scam-6" lesson="l1-checking-a-broker"}
Strong regulators often require brokers to offer retail clients:
- [x] Negative balance protection and limits on leverage
- [ ] Guaranteed profits
- [ ] Unlimited leverage
? Rules such as these limit how much a retail client can lose.
::
::question{id="scam-7" lesson="l1-when-firms-fail"}
Why does it matter that client money is kept segregated?
- [ ] It earns you extra interest
- [x] It keeps your money separate from the firm's own money if the firm fails
- [ ] It lets the broker use it for trading
? Segregation helps clients get money back if the firm goes bust.
::
::question{id="scam-8" lesson="l1-when-firms-fail"}
What is a sensible habit with broker deposits?
- [ ] Keep all your savings with one broker for convenience
- [x] Only deposit what you need for trading, and withdraw profits from time to time
- [ ] Never withdraw, so the account grows faster
? Firms can fail. Limiting what sits with any one firm limits what you could lose.
::
