::question{id="gap-1" lesson="l1-weekend-and-news-gaps"}
A price gap is:
- [x] A jump where price moves from one level to another with no trading in between
- [ ] The space between two candles on a chart setting
- [ ] The difference between two brokers' fees
? Gaps happen when the market reopens or news hits, so the next available price is far from the last one.
::
::question{id="gap-2" lesson="l1-weekend-and-news-gaps"}
When are gaps most common in forex?
- [ ] In the middle of the London session on a quiet day
- [x] When the market reopens after the weekend, and around big news
- [ ] Never; forex trades 24 hours so it cannot gap
? Forex closes over the weekend, and news can jump prices instantly.
::
::question{id="gap-3" lesson="l1-weekend-and-news-gaps"}
Your stop-loss is at 1.2000. Over the weekend bad news hits and the market opens at 1.1900. Where is your stop most likely filled?
- [ ] Exactly at 1.2000
- [x] Near 1.1900, the first available price
- [ ] It is cancelled and the trade stays open
? A stop becomes a market order. If price jumps past it, you get the next available price.
::
::question{id="gap-4" lesson="l1-weekend-and-news-gaps"}
Which habit reduces your weekend gap risk?
- [ ] Moving your stop further away on Friday
- [x] Cutting position size or closing trades before the weekend
- [ ] Adding to positions late on Friday
? You cannot stop a gap, but you can control how much is exposed to one.
::
::question{id="gap-5" lesson="l1-the-day-the-franc-jumped"}
In January 2015, what did the Swiss National Bank do?
- [ ] Raised interest rates by 5%
- [x] Removed its minimum exchange rate of 1.20 francs per euro
- [ ] Switched Switzerland to the euro
? Removing the floor sent the franc surging in minutes.
::
::question{id="gap-6" lesson="l1-the-day-the-franc-jumped"}
Why did many traders lose more than their stop-losses that day?
- [x] Prices jumped so fast that stops were filled far beyond their levels
- [ ] Brokers deleted all stop-loss orders
- [ ] The market closed for a week
? With almost no buyers or sellers in between, orders filled at much worse prices.
::
::question{id="gap-7" lesson="l1-the-day-the-franc-jumped"}
After the franc move, some clients ended with negative balances. What does that mean?
- [ ] Their accounts were frozen but kept their money
- [x] They lost more than they had deposited and owed the broker money
- [ ] They were refunded in full automatically
? FXCM's clients owed more than $225 million after the move. Negative balance protection exists to prevent this.
::
::question{id="gap-8" lesson="l1-the-day-the-franc-jumped"}
What is the main lesson from the franc's jump?
- [ ] Central banks never surprise markets
- [x] A level that "can't break" can break, so size positions for the gap, not just the stop
- [ ] Stops always protect you fully
? Promises and floors can disappear. Size so that a jump past your stop is survivable.
::
