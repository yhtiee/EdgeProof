::question{id="tc-1" lesson="l1-spreads-and-commissions"}
The spread is:
- [x] The gap between the buy (ask) price and the sell (bid) price
- [ ] The broker's yearly account fee
- [ ] How far price moves in a day
? You buy at the ask and sell at the bid, so every trade starts slightly behind by the spread.
::
::question{id="tc-2" type="numeric" answer="1.2" tolerance="0.05" unit="pips" lesson="l1-spreads-and-commissions"}
EUR/USD shows bid 1.08500 and ask 1.08512. What is the spread in pips?
? 1.08512 − 1.08500 = 0.00012, which is 1.2 pips (one pip is 0.0001).
::
::question{id="tc-3" lesson="l1-spreads-and-commissions"}
A "raw spread" account usually charges:
- [ ] Nothing at all to trade
- [x] A tighter spread plus a fixed commission per lot
- [ ] Only a monthly subscription
? Raw accounts show near-interbank spreads and add a commission, so you still pay on every trade.
::
::question{id="tc-4" lesson="l1-swaps-and-the-triple-swap"}
A swap (rollover fee) is charged or paid when you:
- [ ] Open any trade
- [x] Hold a position open past the daily rollover
- [ ] Close a trade at a loss
? Swaps reflect the interest-rate difference between the two currencies for each night you hold.
::
::question{id="tc-5" lesson="l1-swaps-and-the-triple-swap"}
Why do most brokers charge three nights of swap on Wednesday?
- [ ] Wednesday is the busiest trading day
- [x] Wednesday's rollover settles over the weekend, so it covers Saturday and Sunday too
- [ ] It is a penalty for holding trades mid-week
? Spot trades settle two business days later, so Wednesday's position settles on Monday and carries the weekend's interest.
::
::question{id="tc-6" lesson="l1-how-costs-eat-into-every-trade"}
You aim for 5 pips per trade and pay a 1.5 pip spread plus 0.5 pips of commission. What share of your target goes on costs?
- [ ] 10%
- [x] 40%
- [ ] 75%
? Total cost is 2 pips. 2 ÷ 5 = 40% of the target, so small targets suffer most.
::
::question{id="tc-7" lesson="l1-how-costs-eat-into-every-trade"}
Which trading style is hit hardest by spreads and commissions?
- [x] Many quick trades with small targets
- [ ] A few long trades with large targets
- [ ] Not trading at all
? Costs are roughly fixed per trade, so the smaller the target, the larger the share they take.
::
::question{id="tc-8" lesson="l1-how-costs-eat-into-every-trade"}
A strategy shows a small profit before costs. What should you check before trusting it?
- [ ] Nothing; a profit is a profit
- [x] Whether it is still profitable after spread, commission, swap and slippage
- [ ] Whether the chart looks good on a bigger screen
? Many small edges vanish once realistic costs are included.
::
