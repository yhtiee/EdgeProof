::question{id="ses-1" lesson="l1-the-three-trading-sessions"}
The three main trading sessions are usually named:
- [x] Asia, London and New York
- [ ] Morning, noon and night
- [ ] Sydney, Paris and Chicago
? Trading follows the sun through these three financial centres.
::
::question{id="ses-2" lesson="l1-the-three-trading-sessions"}
When is the forex market usually busiest?
- [ ] In the middle of the Asian session
- [x] When the London and New York sessions overlap
- [ ] Just after the daily rollover
? The overlap brings the most buyers and sellers, so spreads are usually tightest.
::
::question{id="ses-3" lesson="l1-the-three-trading-sessions"}
Which currency pair is usually most active in the Asian session?
- [ ] EUR/GBP
- [x] USD/JPY
- [ ] EUR/CHF
? Tokyo is the main Asian centre, so yen pairs see more trade then.
::
::question{id="ses-4" lesson="l1-the-three-trading-sessions"}
Why does the session matter when you trade?
- [ ] Prices are fixed outside London hours
- [x] Activity, spreads and typical movement change through the day
- [ ] Only one session allows selling
? The same pair can be calm and cheap at one hour, and thin and expensive at another.
::
::question{id="ses-5" lesson="l1-quiet-hours-and-rollover"}
The daily rollover happens around:
- [ ] Midnight in London
- [x] 5 p.m. New York time
- [ ] Noon in Tokyo
? Most brokers roll positions and charge swaps around 5 p.m. New York time.
::
::question{id="ses-6" lesson="l1-quiet-hours-and-rollover"}
What often happens to spreads around the rollover?
- [ ] They shrink to zero
- [x] They widen sharply for a short time
- [ ] Nothing at all
? Many banks step back at that time, so liquidity is thin and spreads widen.
::
::question{id="ses-7" lesson="l1-quiet-hours-and-rollover"}
Your stop is a few pips from price and it's minutes before the rollover. What is the risk?
- [x] A spread spike could trigger your stop without a real price move
- [ ] Your trade will be closed for free
- [ ] The stop is ignored during the rollover
? Sell stops trigger on the bid and buy stops on the ask, so a spread spike can hit tight stops.
::
::question{id="ses-8" lesson="l1-quiet-hours-and-rollover"}
Which times are typically the thinnest in forex?
- [ ] The London and New York overlap
- [x] Around the daily rollover, late Friday and early Sunday
- [ ] The first hour of London
? Liquidity is lowest when the big centres are closed or closing.
::
