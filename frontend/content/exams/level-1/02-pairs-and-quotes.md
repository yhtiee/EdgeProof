::question{id="pq-1" lesson="l1-base-and-quote-currencies"}
In the pair USD/JPY, which is the base currency?
- [x] USD
- [ ] JPY
- [ ] Neither; both are quote currencies
? The base currency is always written first. The price says how many yen one dollar costs.
::
::question{id="pq-2" type="numeric" answer="150" tolerance="0" unit="yen" lesson="l1-base-and-quote-currencies"}
USD/JPY is quoted at 150.00. How many yen does one US dollar cost?
? The quote is the number of units of the quote currency (yen) per one unit of the base (dollar): 150.
::
::question{id="pq-3" lesson="l1-base-and-quote-currencies"}
EUR/GBP is quoted at 0.8500. What does that mean?
- [ ] One pound costs 0.85 euros
- [x] One euro costs 0.85 pounds
- [ ] Euros and pounds are worth the same
? Base first: one euro (base) costs 0.85 pounds (quote).
::
::question{id="pq-4" lesson="l1-major-and-minor-pairs"}
What makes a pair a "major"?
- [ ] It moves more than 100 pips a day
- [x] It includes the US dollar paired with another big, heavily traded currency
- [ ] It is only traded during the London session
? Majors are the most traded pairs, each with the US dollar on one side, such as EUR/USD, USD/JPY and GBP/USD.
::
::question{id="pq-5" lesson="l1-major-and-minor-pairs"}
Which of these is a minor (cross) pair?
- [ ] EUR/USD
- [ ] USD/CHF
- [x] EUR/GBP
? Crosses pair two major currencies without the US dollar.
::
::question{id="pq-6" lesson="l1-exotic-currency-pairs"}
Which pair is an exotic?
- [ ] AUD/USD
- [x] USD/TRY
- [ ] GBP/JPY
? Exotics pair a major currency with one from a smaller or emerging economy, such as the Turkish lira.
::
::question{id="pq-7" lesson="l1-exotic-currency-pairs"}
Compared with majors, exotic pairs usually have:
- [x] Wider spreads and sharper jumps
- [ ] Tighter spreads and calmer moves
- [ ] No swap charges at all
? Less trading means wider spreads, higher costs and more gap risk.
::
::question{id="pq-8" lesson="l1-major-and-minor-pairs"}
Why do most beginners do better starting with major pairs?
- [ ] Majors always trend upward
- [x] They are the cheapest to trade and the most liquid
- [ ] Brokers give extra leverage on them only to beginners
? High liquidity keeps spreads tight and fills predictable, so costs eat less of each trade.
::
