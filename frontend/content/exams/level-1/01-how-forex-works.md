::question{id="hfw-1" lesson="l1-money-and-exchange-rates"}
An exchange rate tells you:
- [ ] How much interest a currency pays each year
- [x] How much of one currency you need to buy one unit of another
- [ ] How many traders are buying a currency today
? An exchange rate is a price: one currency expressed in units of another.
::
::question{id="hfw-2" lesson="l1-money-and-exchange-rates"}
EUR/USD moves from 1.1000 to 1.1200. What happened?
- [x] The euro got stronger against the dollar
- [ ] The dollar got stronger against the euro
- [ ] Both currencies lost value equally
? One euro now buys more dollars (1.12 instead of 1.10), so the euro strengthened.
::
::question{id="hfw-3" lesson="l1-what-a-forex-trade-is"}
When you buy EUR/USD, you are:
- [ ] Buying dollars and selling euros
- [x] Buying euros and selling dollars at the same time
- [ ] Lending euros to your broker
? Every forex trade is two currencies at once. Buying the pair buys the first (base) currency and sells the second.
::
::question{id="hfw-4" lesson="l1-what-a-forex-trade-is"}
You sell GBP/USD. Which outcome makes you money?
- [ ] GBP/USD rises
- [x] GBP/USD falls
- [ ] GBP/USD stays exactly the same for a week
? Selling profits when the price falls: you sold the pound high and can buy it back cheaper.
::
::question{id="hfw-5" lesson="l1-the-over-the-counter-market"}
"Over the counter" means the forex market:
- [ ] Trades only in a single building in London
- [x] Has no single central exchange; prices are agreed between dealers and their clients
- [ ] Is closed to anyone who is not a bank
? Forex is decentralised. Banks, brokers and platforms quote prices to each other, so quotes can differ slightly between them.
::
::question{id="hfw-6" lesson="l1-the-over-the-counter-market"}
Because forex has no central exchange, two brokers can show:
- [x] Slightly different prices for the same pair at the same moment
- [ ] Completely different currency pairs under the same name
- [ ] Prices only once per hour
? Each dealer sets its own quotes from its liquidity sources, so small differences are normal.
::
::question{id="hfw-7" lesson="l1-who-trades-forex"}
Which group does most of the volume in the forex market?
- [ ] Retail traders on phone apps
- [x] Banks and other large financial institutions
- [ ] Tourists exchanging cash at airports
? Big banks, funds and corporations dominate turnover. Retail traders are a small share of the market.
::
::question{id="hfw-8" lesson="l1-what-moves-currency-prices"}
Which of these most directly moves a currency's price over time?
- [ ] The colour of the country's banknotes
- [x] Changes in interest rates and expectations about the economy
- [ ] The number of people who follow the currency on social media
? Interest rates, growth, inflation and shifts in expectations drive the flow of money between currencies.
::
::question{id="hfw-9" lesson="l1-what-moves-currency-prices"}
A currency usually rises when traders come to expect that:
- [ ] Its central bank will cut interest rates sooner than thought
- [x] Its central bank will raise interest rates sooner than thought
- [ ] Its country will hold an election
? Higher expected rates make holding the currency more attractive, which tends to lift its price.
::
