::question{id="jo-1" lesson="l3-what-to-record-in-a-journal"}
Which facts should every journal entry record?
- [x] Pair, direction, entry, stop, target, size, exit and result in R
- [ ] Only the profit or loss in money
- [ ] Only the trades that won
? Full facts let you analyse what actually happened.
::
::question{id="jo-2" lesson="l3-what-to-record-in-a-journal"}
Besides the facts, a good journal also records:
- [x] Why you took the trade, whether it followed your rules, and how you felt
- [ ] Other traders' opinions
- [ ] Only your best trades
? Reasons and emotions explain the results and reveal patterns.
::
::question{id="jo-3" lesson="l3-what-to-record-in-a-journal"}
Why record losing trades as carefully as winners?
- [ ] You shouldn't; losers aren't useful
- [x] Losers often reveal rule-breaking or weak setups you need to fix
- [ ] Brokers need them for tax
? Your losses hold some of your most useful lessons.
::
::question{id="jo-4" lesson="l3-what-to-record-in-a-journal"}
When is the best time to write a journal entry?
- [x] As close to the trade as possible, while the details are fresh
- [ ] At the end of the month from memory
- [ ] Only when you remember to
? Memory changes quickly, especially after a loss.
::
::question{id="jo-5" lesson="l3-learning-from-your-journal"}
After 50 entries, you notice most losses came from trades taken outside your plan. What should you do?
- [x] Focus on stopping off-plan trades, since they cost you the most
- [ ] Change your strategy completely
- [ ] Trade more to make up for it
? Fixing rule-breaking often improves results more than changing the strategy.
::
::question{id="jo-6" lesson="l3-learning-from-your-journal"}
Why compare results of trades that followed your rules with trades that didn't?
- [x] It shows what your rule-breaking actually costs
- [ ] It makes the journal longer
- [ ] It proves the market is random
? Seeing the cost in R makes the case for discipline concrete.
::
::question{id="jo-7" lesson="l3-learning-from-your-journal"}
Your journal shows you lose most often in the last hour of your session. A good next step is:
- [x] Test a rule that stops trading earlier
- [ ] Trade more in that hour to get better
- [ ] Ignore it; it's probably random
? Journals reveal patterns you can turn into rules, then check with more data.
::
::question{id="jo-8" lesson="l3-learning-from-your-journal"}
A "never-again list" is:
- [x] A short list of specific mistakes you have decided not to repeat
- [ ] A list of pairs you'll never trade
- [ ] A list of brokers to avoid
? It turns lessons from your journal into simple reminders you read before each session.
::
