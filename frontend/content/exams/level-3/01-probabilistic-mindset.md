::question{id="pm-1" lesson="l3-five-truths-about-any-trade"}
"Anything can happen" means:
- [x] Any trade can lose, however good the setup looks
- [ ] Markets are completely unpredictable, so no edge exists
- [ ] You should take every trade you see
? Even strong setups lose sometimes. Accepting that keeps you from overconfidence.
::
::question{id="pm-2" lesson="l3-five-truths-about-any-trade"}
Do you need to know what happens next to make money trading?
- [ ] Yes, you must predict each trade correctly
- [x] No, you need an edge and the discipline to repeat it over many trades
- [ ] Only for the first few trades
? Profit comes from repeating a positive-expectancy process, not from predicting single outcomes.
::
::question{id="pm-3" lesson="l3-five-truths-about-any-trade"}
A chart looks just like one from last week that ended in a big rally. What should you assume?
- [ ] It will end the same way
- [x] Every moment is new; it may or may not end the same way
- [ ] It will do the opposite
? Similar-looking setups only shift the odds. They don't repeat the outcome.
::
::question{id="pm-4" lesson="l3-five-truths-about-any-trade"}
An edge is best described as:
- [ ] A guarantee that the next trade wins
- [x] A slightly higher chance of a good outcome, which shows over many trades
- [ ] A secret indicator
? An edge is a tilt in the odds, not a certainty.
::
::question{id="pm-5" lesson="l3-accepting-the-risk-before-you-enter"}
"Accepting the risk" before a trade means:
- [x] Knowing your stop and your loss in money, and being genuinely fine if it's hit
- [ ] Hoping the trade won't reach your stop
- [ ] Not placing a stop so you can't lose
? If you've truly accepted the loss before entry, a losing trade won't push you into emotional decisions.
::
::question{id="pm-6" lesson="l3-accepting-the-risk-before-you-enter"}
You feel anxious watching a trade after entry. What does that often mean?
- [ ] The trade is about to win
- [x] The size is too big, or you hadn't really accepted the risk
- [ ] You should add to the position
? Anxiety is a signal to reduce size and define the loss in advance.
::
::question{id="pm-7" lesson="l3-accepting-the-risk-before-you-enter"}
Which question should you answer before every entry?
- [ ] How much could I win?
- [x] Where am I wrong, and how much will that cost me?
- [ ] What do others on social media think?
? Defining the loss first keeps every trade within your plan.
::
::question{id="pm-8" lesson="l3-accepting-the-risk-before-you-enter"}
You've lost four trades in a row, all within your plan. A probabilistic thinker would:
- [ ] Assume the strategy is broken and abandon it
- [x] Recognise that streaks are expected and keep following the plan at the same size
- [ ] Double the size on the next trade
? Streaks happen to good strategies. Judge the plan over a larger sample.
::
